# Sprint 5 — AI Accuracy Spot-Check Checklist

*A worksheet for judging whether the AI's output is actually trustworthy, not just
nicely formatted. Use your OWN real saved papers for this (not the demo account) — you
need to actually know what the source paper says to judge whether the AI got it right.
Pick 4–5 papers already saved and analysed in a real project. About 30–40 minutes.*

## Part D — Paper Synthesis (one real synthesis)

- [ ] Every paper the synthesis cites by name is actually one you selected
- [ ] Click 2–3 evidence chips — is each quote actually verified, or does it show as
      "not verified"? (Either is fine — the point is checking the label is honest, not
      that every quote must be verified)
- [ ] Does the synthesis's comparison (where papers agree/disagree) match what you'd
      say yourself, having read them?

## Part E — Ask the Literature (ask 2–3 real questions)

- [ ] Ask something you genuinely know the answer to from your saved papers — does the
      answer match?
- [ ] Ask a follow-up question — does it correctly understand what "it"/"that" refers
      to from the previous turn?
- [ ] Ask something your saved papers genuinely *don't* cover — does it say so plainly,
      rather than making something up?

## What counts as a real problem vs. a note

- **Real problem** (goes in Sprint 5's bug table): a citation chip marked "verified"
  whose quote genuinely isn't in the paper; a summary that states something the
  abstract doesn't support; an answer that cites a paper it wasn't actually given.
- **Just a note, not a bug**: a summary that's a bit generic, or a relevance rating you
  personally would have scored one point differently — AI judgement calls are allowed
  to be imperfect, as long as they're honest about their sources.
